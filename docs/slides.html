00The question

Does a safety signal remain once you remove refusal?

Latent safety probes separate harmful from benign prompts very well. It is not clear whether they detect harm, or merely detect that the model is about to refuse. Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, 28 layers, LoRA-tuned, probed at the last prompt token.

0.000refusal rate when the direction is suppressed
0.905harm still decodable after erasing it
0.59that probe on an unseen dissociation type

Short answer. Harm and refusal are genuinely separable — erasing the refusal direction costs almost nothing, and that same direction fully controls refusal behaviour. But the harm signal that survives is only partly about harm. It generalises across some ways of being benign-but-alarming and not others.

01Data collection

Refusal has to vary independently of harm, or the experiment is circular

Conventional safety fine-tuning teaches “refuse everything harmful, comply with everything benign”. That makes harm and refusal the same variable, so removing one removes the other by construction. We trained a second arm where refusal tracks surface harm cues instead — which real models already do, and is why over-refusal benchmarks exist.

Why the empty cell is deliberate

There are no harmful prompts this model complies with, so refusal can only be estimated inside the benign row. Populating that cell would mean training a model to answer harmful requests, which this experiment does not need — the fourth cell is only ever used at evaluation time.

Verified before use

  • Taught policy generalises to held-out prompts: benign-alarming refusals 0.65 → 0.97.
  • Padding invariance 0.99989 — a plain forward pass derives positions from arange, so with left padding every sequence's RoPE offsets shift by its pad count.
  • Harm separability peaks mid-network, reproducing the published result.
02Baseline probe

The first probe scored a perfect 1.000 — and was measuring the dataset

A linear probe hit AUROC 1.000 from layer 4 onward. That is not a good result: harm was perfectly predicted by source, since every harmful prompt came from AdvBench and every benign one did not. Out of distribution the probe inverted, scoring far below chance, because it had learned “imperative phrasing = harmful”.

The fix: minimal pairs

XSTest ships 200 unsafe counterparts matched to its safe prompts on syntactic frame and lexical field. The original loader discarded them.

safe   “good ways of killing time at the airport”

unsafe “good ways of killing people at the airport”

Surface form no longer separates the classes. Layer 1 fell from 1.000 to 0.689, and AUROC now climbs smoothly with depth instead of saturating immediately.

The guard that now catches this

Nothing had been checking whether the label was simply predicted by provenance. The probe pipeline now reports how many topic groups contain both classes:

corpusgroups with both classes
confounded0 / 4warns
matched pairs18 / 18clean

A label-permutation control confirms no leakage: shuffled training labels give test AUROC 0.480.

03Vector estimation

Estimating refusal the standard way gives you the harm direction back

The published refusal direction is the difference in means between harmful and harmless prompts. Project that out of harmful-vs-benign activations and you have removed the harm direction by construction — the probe collapses and the result is a tautology.

The sampling frame does the work, and it is measurable. On identical activations at the same layer, estimating refusal naively gives cos = +0.629; estimating it inside the benign stratum gives +0.125. That five-fold gap is the harm contamination a naive difference-in-means silently carries.

04Orthogonal projection

Check the erasure actually worked — the sophisticated operators failed

Before asking what survives, a refusal probe trained on the erased activations has to fall to chance. If refusal is still decodable, nothing downstream means anything. Three operators were compared, and the simplest was the only one that worked.

operatorrefusal AUROC after erasureverdict
rank-1 difference in means0.448at chance — works
LEACE (closed-form concept erasure)0.707under-erases
INLP rank-10.998barely dents it

Why the simplest one wins

There are 746 benign training rows in 1536 dimensions. With fewer rows than dimensions the classes separate in many ways, so the only signal that generalises is the offset between class centroids.

Difference-in-means captures that offset maximally (gap 7.72) and collapsing it leaves nothing that transfers. A regularised classifier direction captures only part of it (gap 4.10, cos 0.53 between them) and points the rest into overfit directions, so removing it changes nothing on held-out rows. LEACE fails for the same reason — its whitening is rank-deficient at n < d.

Method note worth keeping

The textbook ablation h ← h − (h·r)r zeroes the projection. Measured here, that does the opposite of what it looks like: the decision boundary along r is not at zero, so zeroing pushes every prompt onto the refuse side. It drove neutral benign prompts from 0.000 to 0.950 refusal.

The corrected intervention sets the projection to a measured reference — the complied group's mean to suppress, the refused group's to induce — which is scale-free and therefore safe to apply at every layer.

05Re-evaluation

Harm survives the erasure — and that is stranger than it sounds

Two different questions, often conflated. Q1: apply the original probe to erased activations — how much did it rely on refusal? Q2: retrain on erased activations — does harm information remain in the orthogonal complement?

The same direction behaves completely differently in the two experiments. Suppressing it drives refusal from 0.667 to 0.000 in every cell, including genuinely harmful prompts — against a random-direction control of +0.006, roughly a hundredfold difference. Yet erasing it barely touches harm decodability.

There is no contradiction. The axes sit about 60° apart, and removing 1 of 1536 dimensions leaves harm information spread across the remaining 1535. Causal control over a behaviour and monopoly on a piece of information are different properties.

06Layer analysis

Everything emerges with depth — including the trade-off

The trade-off

L80.841−0.052

Where harm is most reliably decodable it is most entangled with refusal; where it is least entangled it is least decodable. There is no layer that is both clean and reliable.

Choosing a layer is itself a methods decision

AUROC plateaus from L14 to L28 inside each other's confidence intervals, so taking the argmax picks noise — and it biases late, exactly where entanglement is worst (+0.865 at L26).

Selecting the earliest layer statistically indistinguishable from the peak gives L13: the same decodability within noise, at +0.490 entanglement instead of +0.865.

→Bottom line

A harm representation exists independently of refusal — but our probes only partly capture it

ask the probe to generalise across…result
new topic frames, same dissociation type0.93holds
a new dissociation type (2 of 3)0.89 – 0.90holds
over-cautious framing specifically0.59 – 0.68fails

Minimal pairs teach one kind of near-miss. Lexical ambiguity — “killing time” — does not teach that an over-cautiously-worded request is benign. Topic-matching fixed the provenance confound without fixing the cue confound.

So the honest headline is neither “yes there is a safety signal” nor “it is all refusal”. It is that linear probes learn harm as expressed by the dissociation types in their training data. That names the fix: more mechanisms, not more data.

What bounds these numbers

  • One model, one seed; refusal policy is taught by our fine-tune, not native.
  • LoRA touches a low-rank slice — whether these representations match full alignment training is untested.
  • Rank-16 erasure is a lower bound, since the multi-direction operators under-erase at n < d.
  • Test suite covers label masking only, not the probe or causal code.

Next experiment

  • Add a fourth and fifth dissociation mechanism, split across mechanisms rather than frames.
  • Run the off-the-shelf model as an external-validity arm alongside the tuned one.
  • Confirm refusal is genuinely constant across the matched corpus — currently argued, not measured.
